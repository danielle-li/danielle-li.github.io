  <p><span class="font-medium">Abstract: </span> (Under preparation for law review audience) This Article examines how modern AI systems are built from "knowledge data" generated by workers: records of decisions, judgments, and interactions that capture human expertise.  Under the current legal status quo, this knowledge becomes firm-owned capital once codified into AI systems, facilitating productivity gains but raising risks of worker exploitation and resistance. We introduce a framework based on two dimensions---replicability and substitutability---to analyze how different workers may be impacted by the codification of their work knowledge. We then propose knowledge guilds, collective data governance institutions that grant workers entitlements over the privacy, control, and payment for their knowledge data, as a means of enabling worker cooperation in AI development while ensuring that they share in the gains of automation
